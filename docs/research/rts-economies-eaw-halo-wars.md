# Economies in Empire at War and the Halo Wars games

Researched 2026-11-06. Status: **research only**; nothing is built from it. The question: how do *Star Wars: Empire at War* (2006, with *Forces of Corruption*), *Halo Wars* (2009) and *Halo Wars 2* (2017) run their economies? Which resources exist, what each pays for, where each comes from, what caps and gates sit beside them, whether there is upkeep, and what losing territory does to income. The aim is to inform how Halo Battles splits Credits, Materials and Alloys, after a playtester asked for resources "separated into use cases".

## Verdict

- **None of the three games uses three spendable resources.** Empire at War has one (Credits), Halo Wars has one (Supplies) and Halo Wars 2 has two (Supply and Power). Halo Battles already has more currencies than any of them.
- **Their other limits are not currencies.** Each game pairs its one or two currencies with gates you can't spend:
  - a **unit cap** (EaW's galactic Unit Capacity and tactical population cap, Halo Wars' Population);
  - a **tech level** (bought with Credits in EaW, a count of Reactors in Halo Wars, base upgrades in Halo Wars 2);
  - **build slots** (2–8 land slots per planet in EaW; 3, 5 or 7 slots per base in both Halo Wars games).

  These caps and gates do much of the "separation" a third resource would otherwise do.
- **Halo Wars 2 is the only split, and it splits by activity, not by unit type.** Supply trains units and builds structures. Power pays for research, building upgrades and base tiers. Most prices use one resource:
  - core units are Supply only;
  - research is Power only;
  - production buildings are Supply only.

  Only advanced units, heroes, some Leader Powers and the Firebase mix the two, and for most units that mix is a small Power top-up (a Scorpion is 650 Supply + 50 Power).
- **None of the three charges upkeep.** I found no running cost in any manual or official guide. Army size is limited by the population cap instead. A few running costs exist: Covenant leader powers in Halo Wars drain resources per second, and FoC corruption and smugglers siphon income.
- **Income grows with territory, with something to stop it running away:**
  - EaW pays per planet, adds mining facilities and trade-route bonuses, and caps savings at 10,000 Credits per planet held.
  - Halo Wars has diminishing returns per Supply Pad.
  - Halo Wars 2 raises the cost of each extra Generator.
  - Both Halo Wars games force a choice between economy buildings and production buildings in the same few slots.
- **Losing territory costs the income and buildings on it, but never research.**
  - EaW: the planet's income stops, and the new owner gains the planet's advantages.
  - Halo Wars: losing a Reactor lowers your tech level, but upgrades already bought stay (Prima guide).
  - Halo Wars 2: the tech level never drops (Prima guide).
- **Nothing refines one resource into another.** The only conversions are selling or recycling (EaW sells for credits, Halo Wars 2 refunds half) and Halo Wars 2's allied gifts at a loss (300 spent to give 200).

## Star Wars: Empire at War (2006) and Forces of Corruption

Source: the official LucasArts manuals for EaW and *Forces of Corruption*, in one upload on Scribd ([EaW + FoC manual](https://www.scribd.com/doc/55650373/Star-Wars-Empire-at-War-Manual-En)). Page numbers below are the printed ones. The upload is a third-party host, but the text is the manual's own, including LucasArts's trademark and warranty pages. Wording may differ slightly from the printed copy.

### Galactic layer

- **One resource: Credits.** "Building structures and units requires credits. At the end of each galactic day, you will earn credits from all of the planets you control" (EaW p. 13). In Galactic Conquest, Credits also pay for tech: the Empire builds a Research Facility and buys "Upgrade Imperial Tech" (p. 26).
- **Where Credits come from** (p. 13, "What should I do to improve my economy?"):
  - each planet's daily credit value;
  - "Build more mining facilities on your planets to greatly increase their daily credit value";
  - "Capture planets connected by trade routes for increased profit". If you hold both ends of a route, "you gain a bonus to your credit income" (p. 15);
  - mission bonuses;
  - smugglers that "steal credits from an enemy planet" each galactic day (pp. 13, 38);
  - selling units or structures (p. 13);
  - "instant credits" for conquering a planet (p. 5).
- **A savings cap that grows with territory:** "You can store up to 10,000 credits for each planet you control. You cannot earn more credits than your planets can support" (p. 13).
- **Unit Capacity is the army cap, and it comes from territory.** "The total number of units you can command throughout the galaxy is restricted by the number of planets and space stations under your control. When you reach your galactic population cap, no new units can be constructed" (p. 10). "Each planet you control adds to the total … Building or upgrading a space station … [increases] your Unit Capacity", and "Some units cost more unit capacity points than others" (p. 11). Planet tooltips list a **population cost** and **population bonus** (p. 7).
- **Build slots:**
  - "Each planet has between two and eight land slots", and some planets have none (p. 13).
  - In space there is one space station per planet, upgradable to a per-planet maximum level, plus "two other slots for structures" (p. 13).
  - Each planet can hold at most ten land units (p. 12).
- **Tech level is a separate gate.** "Your technology level determines which units and structures you can build" (p. 10). The Empire **buys** tech with Credits at a Research Facility. The Rebels **steal** it with R2-D2 and C-3PO, and a level rises when "all of the technologies in [a] group have been stolen" (p. 26). Higher tech allows higher space-station levels, which raise Unit Capacity (p. 26).
- **Upkeep:** the manual describes none. The only per-day effects are income and smugglers. I found no running cost for units or buildings in the base game.
- **Losing a planet:** its daily credits stop, and the attacker who wins both space and land "gains all advantages that the planet provides" (p. 15). That presumably includes the mining facilities still standing, but the manual doesn't say so. A planet destroyed by the Death Star "can no longer be used to build land structures" (p. 13).

### Tactical battles

- **No income during a battle.** "You enter tactical combat with the same number of total credits that you had on the Galactic Map. Galactic time stops during tactical combat, so you will not earn daily credits" (p. 21).
- During a battle, Credits come from capturing a resource facility, from secondary objectives and from selling build-pad emplacements. They pay for defender upgrades and build-pad turrets (p. 21).
- **Population caps in battle are separate from Unit Capacity:**
  - space battles: "Population cap is 20";
  - land battles: the attacker's cap comes from the reinforcement points they hold, and the defender's "is always 10" (p. 16).
- Garrison units, indigenous forces and build-pad emplacements don't count toward the cap (pp. 21, 24, 40).

### Skirmish

- "Each player receives a steady flow of credits but can increase their income by capturing strategic points on the map" (p. 5). You raise income "by capturing mining build sites and resource pads" (p. 25).
- Resource Pads "mine raw materials to sell off for credits. They are not as efficient as full-sized Mining Facilities" (p. 40). That is flavor text, not a second resource.
- Tech level is bought at the command center (p. 25).

### Forces of Corruption

- **Still one resource.** The new Zann Consortium earns and spends Credits like the others. The *Faction Differentiation Chart* lists income as "Planetary Control" for all three factions. The secondary source is "Mining Facilities" for the Empire and Rebels and "Corruption" for the Consortium. Tech comes from building (Empire), theft (Rebels) or the **Black Market** (Consortium) (FoC p. 32).
- **Corruption is the closest thing to a running cost in the series.** It uses Credits as a mission fee:
  - every corruption option needs "Credits available to expend on the operation";
  - *Kidnapping* pays "a large sum of credits";
  - *Racketeering* gives "a larger stream of credits than normal";
  - smugglers "do not expire on corrupted worlds" (FoC pp. 4–6, 10).
- **Black Market** prices vary with the rival factions' tech levels (FoC p. 6).
- **Skirmish change:** Mining Facilities moved onto capturable **Outpost** build pads as "a universal build option" that "Produces a Mining Facility income stream" (FoC p. 8).

## Halo Wars (2009)

Sources:

- The official Xbox 360 *Planetary Operations Manual* ([copy hosted by a third party](https://nuangel.net/pcdownloads/xboxmanuals/HaloWars_MNL_EN-US.pdf)), cited by printed page spread.
- Prima's official guide ([Base Construction Tactics](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/basics/base-construction-tactics), [Firebase Buildings](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/firebase-buildings), [The Command Base](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/the-command-base)).

There's no strategic layer. Everything happens inside one match.

- **One currency: resources, or Supplies.** "Resources are required to build units, buildings, Turrets, and research upgrades; they also power many special attacks and weapons" (manual pp. 14–15). Prima lists the same uses: "buildings, troops, upgrades, and Covenant Leader Powers" ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/basics/base-construction-tactics)).
- **Where Supplies come from:**
  - **Supply Pads** (UNSC) or **Warehouses** (Covenant), "your primary and most efficient way to collect resources";
  - crates "dropped on the battlefield", which infantry or scouts collect (manual pp. 14–15);
  - captured Forerunner structures "that act as Supply Pads" ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/firebase-buildings)).
- **The Heavy Supply Pad / Blessed Warehouse upgrade "increases resources gathered over time"** (manual pp. 24–25, 38–39). Prima puts it at "40 percent more proficient".
- **Diminishing returns.** "Supply Pads have diminishing returns; the more you build, the less proficient they all become". Prima recommends 5–7 pads ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/basics/base-construction-tactics)). Prima's worked example is inconsistent: it says 5→10 pads adds "50 percent" but shows 36.4→72.8.
- **Slots are the real trade-off.** "A key strategical consideration, however, is balancing the number of Supply Pads or Warehouses versus other facilities you must build to train infantry, vehicles, or aircraft" (manual pp. 14–15). A Firebase has 3 building sites, a Station 5 and a Fortress 7 (manual pp. 22–23). "There are only a few base locations on any map" (pp. 12–13).
- **Tech level is a building count, not a currency:**
  - UNSC: "Upgrades and some units may require up to four Reactor technology units". An Advanced Reactor "counts as two tech levels" (manual pp. 24–25).
  - Covenant: the Temple gives "up to three Temple technology levels" (pp. 38–39).
  - Each extra Reactor costs 250 more Supplies ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/firebase-buildings)).
- **Population cap:** "Supply chain and other factors limit the number of units available in the field … Certain upgrades can increase your maximum population … Some larger units cost more than one population" (manual pp. 12–13).
  - The UNSC *Reinforcements* upgrade "Adds +10 … from 30 to 40" ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/firebase-buildings)).
  - Halopedia gives a starting cap of 30 for the UNSC and 40 for the Covenant, with maxima of 50 and 60 if a map's life-support pod is held ([Population](https://www.halopedia.org/Population), **secondary**).
  - In Deathmatch, "teams gain population by claiming bases" (manual pp. 16–17).
- **Upkeep:** none for units or buildings. The one running cost: "The Covenant Leaders' unique powers cost additional resources on a per-second basis" (manual pp. 32–33).
- **Losing territory:**
  - If the Command Center falls, "you lose the entire base and cannot rebuild it for a short time", and with it every pad and Reactor on the base (manual pp. 14–15).
  - Losing a Reactor drops the tech level (pp. 24–25). A destroyed Temple "resets to zero, although it will be restored … if another Temple is built" (pp. 38–39).
  - **Upgrades already bought survive.** Prima advises building Reactors, buying upgrades, then demolishing Reactors "without losing the ability to build upgraded Scorpions" ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/firebase-buildings)). This settles a point left open in [tech-holding-in-strategy-games.md](tech-holding-in-strategy-games.md).
  - Recycling a building returns "around half the building's value" ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-eguide/factions/the-command-base)).

## Halo Wars 2 (2017)

Source: Prima's official guide (*Halo Wars 2* eGuide). Some sections are open, and the rest need a free account, but their tables loaded in full. Cost tables have the columns Tech Level, Population, Supply, Power and Time.

- **Two currencies, split by activity.** From the HUD page ([The HUD](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/multiplayer-basics/the-hud)):
  - "Supply is the main resource used in training units and is not used in research."
  - "Power is used mainly for research and upgrading buildings. Some units do require a small amount of Power for creation."
- **Three more things to manage, none of them spent like a currency** (same page):
  - **Population**, a cap;
  - **Tech Level**, a gate: "To upgrade your Tech Level, you must upgrade your base!";
  - **Leader Points**, earned from XP and spent one at a time on Leader Powers ([Leaders overview](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/leaders/overview)).
- **What costs what** (Prima's tables):

  | Warthog, Kodiak, Hornet, Nightingale | 250 / 500 / 375 / 400 | — | [Vehicles](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/units/vehicles/unsc-vehicles), [Air](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/units/air-units/unsc-air-units) |

  In short, **training and construction are Supply, and research and tiers are Power**. Mixed prices sit on specialist and heavy units. Some active Leader Powers cost both: Archer Missiles is 175 + 175, and ODST Drop is 600 Supply plus 12 population.
- **Where each comes from** ([UNSC economy](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/economy-structures/unsc-economy-structures), [Banished economy](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/economy-structures/banished-economy-structures)):
  - **Supply Pad / Harvester:** 150 Supply, makes 2.7 Supply/s. The Heavy / Augmented upgrade costs 200 *Power* and makes 4.3/s.
  - **Generator / Power Extractor:** makes 3 Power/s. The Advanced / Augmented upgrade costs 300 Power and makes 6/s. "Upgrading an economy structure doubles its effectiveness, but a single economy structure can only be upgraded once" ([About economy structures](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/economy-structures/about-economy-structures)). The pads' 2.7 → 4.3 is less than double, so that line fits Generators better.
  - **Rising cost:** "For each Generator you own, the cost of constructing a new Generator increases by 300 Supply" (200, 500, 800 …). "If an existing Generator … is destroyed, the cost … is lowered by 300 Supply."
  - **Map sources:** captured Resource Nodes give "Power at roughly half the rate of a standard Generator" to the whole team. One-time Supply and Power pickups are also scattered on the map ([Map Features](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/multiplayer-basics/multiplayer-atlas/map-features)).
  - Prima's campaign advice: "the majority of Supply comes via your base", while Nodes give Power ([Game Phases](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/getting-started/game-phases)).
- **Note the cross-dependency.** Each currency is needed to grow the other:
  - Generators are bought with Supply;
  - Supply Pad upgrades and base tiers are bought with Power.
- **Slots and tiers:**
  - The base upgrades "unlock slots for additional structures" and raise the Tech Level ([About Bases](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/bases/about-bases)): 3 slots on a Firebase, 5 on a Combat Station, 7 on a Command Center or Headquarters ([UNSC Bases](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/bases/unsc-bases)).
  - Minibases have 1 or 2 slots, "cannot be upgraded", and have no research or turrets ([Minibases](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/minibases/about-minibases)).
- **Population:**
  - Halopedia gives 80 at the start and 120 at most ([Population](https://www.halopedia.org/Population), **secondary**). Prima's Armory table fits this: *Reinforcements I/II* add 20 each, for 750 and 1250 Power.
  - Units cost 2 (Jackrabbit) to 40 (Condor) population.
  - In Strongholds mode, "Each Stronghold increases your total Population count by five" ([Game Modes](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/multiplayer-basics/game-modes)).
- **Upkeep:** none found. Active Leader Powers have a per-use price and a cooldown.
- **Losing territory:**
  - "If a base falls, all the structures attached to it are also destroyed", but "Your Tech Level never decreases" ([About Bases](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/bases/about-bases)). A lost Generator makes the next one cheaper.
  - Recycling returns half the value at full health ([Structures overview](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/overview)).
- **Transfer, not conversion.** In team modes, economy buildings can "Give Supplies" or "Give Power": 200 to an ally for a 300 cost ([About economy structures](https://primagames.com/eguides/halo-wars-2-eguide/structures/economy-structures/about-economy-structures)). Nothing turns Supply into Power.

## What this suggests for Halo Battles' three resources

All of the following are **suggestions**, not decisions.

### Keep three, or cut to two?

- **No close precedent uses three.** The genre's habit is one or two currencies plus non-spendable gates. Halo Battles already has its gates (Command Points, Building Slots, Techs, Sprawl), so a third currency is extra weight rather than the only lever.
- **Three can still work if each one has a job a player can say in four words, and its own source.** The proposed split (Mines → Materials, Supply Pads → Credits, Foundries → Alloys) gives each resource its own building, which is more than HW2 does. Keep three only if the playtest shows players can read it. Otherwise merge Materials and Alloys and drop the Foundry, which gives HW2's two-currency shape.

### What each resource should pay for

HW2 splits **by activity** (make things vs improve things). The Stellaris-style proposal splits **by unit type** (infantry vs vehicles vs buildings). The trade-off:

- **By unit type** (the proposal) keeps every price to one resource. But it ties your counter options to an economy building. A player with no Foundry can't answer an air push with Wolverines. HW2 avoids this: Warthogs, Hornets and Kodiaks are Supply only, and only specialist units add Power.
- **By activity** (HW2), mapped onto Halo Battles:
  - **Credits:** training units (all classes), plus upkeep and the Exchange. This matches HW2's Supply.
  - **Materials:** buildings. Halo Battles has a construction economy HW2 doesn't separate, so this is new.
  - **Alloys:** Tech research and building upgrades, plus a small top-up on heavy and elite units (Scorpion, Wraith, Spartans). This matches HW2's Power.
- **A middle path:** use the proposal as written, but price **basic** vehicles and aircraft in Credits and keep Alloys for heavy and advanced ones. Every unit class then stays reachable without a Foundry, and Alloys still mean "the good stuff".

### One resource per price, or mixes?

- **Aim for one resource per price for most items, and allow a deliberate, small second one on top-tier items.** That's what HW2 does: about 80% of its tables are single-resource, and the mixed ones are Supply plus a little Power, never even splits (the Condor super unit is the exception).
- A second resource on a price should read as a **premium** ("+50 Alloys"), not a recipe. Today's "C + M + A" on a Ghost is the pattern none of these games uses.

### Upkeep

- **None of the three has upkeep.** They limit armies with a population cap and limit savings with storage caps (EaW) or no cap at all (Halo Wars).
- Halo Battles has both Upkeep and Command Points, so army size is limited twice. Options:
  1. **Keep upkeep in Credits only** (as the proposal says), and drop Alloy upkeep. That gives one drain to watch and one reason for Supply Pads.
  2. **Drop unit upkeep and lean on Command Points**, as all three games do. Building upkeep could stay as a light Credits drain.
- Either way, **Credits as the single upkeep currency** has precedent in spirit: EaW's one currency pays for everything and the HUD shows daily net income.

### Ideas worth borrowing

- **Tech gates paid in the "improve" currency (HW2).** Base tiers and research cost only Power. If Alloys pay for Techs, Foundries become the research economy.
- **Rising cost per extra refinery (HW2 Generators: +300 each, and cheaper again when one is lost).** Applied to Foundries, this limits Alloy rushing without a hard cap, and softens losses.
- **Diminishing returns on stacking one extractor type (Halo Wars Supply Pads).** An alternative to Sprawl for stopping a one-building economy.
- **A storage cap that grows with territory (EaW: 10,000 per planet).** Halo Battles has flat Stockpile caps. Scaling them per Planet would make territory feel richer and tie losses to stockpile limits.
- **Trade-route bonuses (EaW).** Income for holding *connected* Planets, which fits the galaxy map's lanes.
- **Economy and production share slots (both Halo Wars games).** Halo Battles' 2–4 Building Slots already create this. Keeping extractors in the same slots as Barracks and Foundries is what makes "eco vs army" a real choice.
- **Tech that never drops when a base is lost (HW2), or upgrades kept after the gate goes (Halo Wars).** This matches the Direction B conclusion in the tech-holding note.
- **The Exchange as a poor-rate gift (HW2 gives 200 for 300).** HW2's only transfer runs at a loss, so a poor-rate Exchange fits. Nothing in these games converts resources freely.

## Not verified

- **EaW/FoC:**
  - I read the manuals through a Scribd upload, not a LucasArts-hosted PDF.
  - Planet income numbers, mining-facility yields, tech costs and Unit Capacity values per planet or station level aren't in the manual. Fan sources disagree and are often about mods, so I left them out.
  - Whether a conquered planet's mining facilities pass to the conqueror.
  - Whether the base game has any per-unit upkeep. The manual mentions none.
- **Halo Wars:**
  - The starting population per faction: Prima says 30→40 for the UNSC, and Halopedia (**secondary**) adds 40 for the Covenant and the 50/60 maxima.
  - Whether UNSC leader powers cost Supplies. The manual says resources "power many special attacks", and Prima names only Covenant Leader Powers as a Supply cost.
  - Exact Supply Pad income per second. Prima's diminishing-returns example is internally inconsistent.
- **Halo Wars 2:**
  - Prima's tables are the launch-era guide. Later patches may have changed costs and rates, and Prima itself warns that values "are subject to change".
  - Starting population (80) and the maximum (120) are from Halopedia (**secondary**). The +20/+20 Reinforcements upgrades from Prima fit those numbers but don't state them.
  - Banished unit costs weren't checked, only Banished economy buildings.
- **None of this was tested in play.**
